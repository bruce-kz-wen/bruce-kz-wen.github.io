    Identification and Optimal Reserve Prices in Ascending Auctions, with an Application to Art
    with Kirill Ponomarev (submitted)
    [paper]

    We develop a partial-identification approach for English auctions with private values, unobserved heterogeneity, and a potentially unknown number of bidders. Our framework restricts dependence among valuations away from the limiting cases of independent private values and perfect correlation, yielding sharp, informative bounds on the distribution of the highest valuation and the seller’s expected profit. We formulate reserve-price choice using a minimaxregret criterion and derive tractable solutions to two relaxations of the seller’s problem. We apply the method to a new dataset covering more than 3,500 lots auctioned by Christie’s and Sotheby’s. For modern art sold in New York City with presale estimates between $1 million and $10 million, we find that setting the reserve price 37 percent above the projected value of an unsold lot would increase expected profit by approximately $150,000 to $489,000 per lot, equivalent to 27 to 87 percent of average realized profit.
